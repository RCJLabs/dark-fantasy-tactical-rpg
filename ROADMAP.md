# Roadmap

Ashmark. A mercenary company with no name, taking contracts in the Pale Marches until the realm has to say what they are called.

The game is the browser. A player should be able to install it, take the company from the Toll Road to an ending, and know what the name cost. Unity is a later copy of a finished browser campaign. It is not the next work.

## Where this is heading

An unnamed company earns a name. The marches answer that name. The player can reach an ending on a phone, in landscape, without a guide.

The company stays the character. Coin buys a blade, not a catalog. The three marks stay the only language of a fight. New work makes that language clearer, the camp more alive, and the road longer. It does not add a fourth mark, a loot ladder, or a second set of rules.

## Laws

These stay. A feature that breaks one of them is the wrong feature.

- One step, then a strike or a skill. Watch is the choice to hold the strike.
- Prime lays a mark. Detonate spends it. A mark spent by a different blade than the one who laid it cuts deeper.
- Ashbrand is the name. Hexrot gnaws and pins. Blooddebt pays the spender back.
- The company is the character. A downed sellsword comes back scarred. Enough scars and the marches keep the body.
- Five branches, three forks, five steps. Each branch is its own trade. You walk only one fork of a branch.
- A fight should be readable on the grid. The log is a record, not the only way to know what happened.

## Where it stands

Playable in the browser, in landscape, and installable.

- First table: the Toll Road, Ash Orchard, the Weeping Chapel, Iron Mile, the Naming.
- The Naming speaks the company name and opens the second table. It does not close the book.
- Second table: the Answered Road, Hex Market, the Echo. Three fights, then the end.
- Radiant side work: the Lantern Mere, the Thorn Wife, Saint's Drown, the Grave Wyrm, the Pale Choir.
- Roster of Sera Voss, Marrow, and Isk. Vetch can be bought after the toll. Halden, Bran, and Quill wait further down the first march.
- Turn a contract down and the one after brings another body. The most hurt blade is scarred on the road.
- Prime and detonate. Enemy play. Watch, threat cells, strike motion, health on the token.
- Terrain: forest hides, rubble covers, mire costs grit. The ground is a picture, not only a color.
- Scars on the portrait. Coin, renown, and local saves that refuse to wipe a company.
- Skill trees behind a button. Seventy-five steps. One fork of a branch. A hired knife has no tree.
- Title, a menu of saves, settings, and a loading painting before the field.

The field still fights the screen. Some foes still wear another face. Camp is a choice, not a place. The second table is short. The Echo is an ending, and it is the only one.

## The twenty phases

Work them in order. A phase is done when its last line is true, not when the code exists somewhere.

### 1. The field, finished

The landscape fight is the whole screen. Every tile is visible. Four portraits fit the rail. Menu and sound sit where they cover nothing. The action bar does not eat a rank.

Done when a phone in landscape can finish a fight without a control covering a unit.

### 2. Faces that are theirs

Name-Taker, Debt Cleaver, and Ash Crow get their own pictures. Marrow, Halden, and Bran keep their weapons inside the frame. No neon fringe, no black box, no second figure hiding in the paint.

Done when every named blade and every named foe has one clean sprite.

### 3. The fight you can read

On the token, without the log: who is acting, the mark and the turns left, a pin, the squares that eat a Watch, and the strike number before it is spent. A forecast when you aim. A selected blade is obvious.

Done when a spectator can tell what the company is about to do.

### 4. Four blades, chosen

The company is larger than the field. Before the fight you choose up to four. The ones who sit out are still on the roster, still scar, and still matter at camp. The ground is visible before you lock the choice.

Done when taking the field is a decision, not the whole company poured onto the grid.

### 5. Motion that means something

A step reads as a step. A strike hits the body it names. A fall is not a fade. Health drops on the token while it happens. Marks appear when they are laid, not a second later.

Done when you can follow a round with the sound off and the log closed.

### 6. Camp is a place

Between contracts the company has a screen of its own, full landscape, not a list on the war table. Who is standing, who is hired, who was refused, what the next road is. Coin on the table. One hire, if one is offered.

Done when you can sit at camp and know the next morning without opening a fight.

### 7. Chronicle and graves

Every contract writes a line. Every scar writes a line. A blade on their last life is named before you march. A blade the marches keep gets a grave, and the grave stays in the chronicle.

Done when a lost company can be read after it is gone.

### 8. The name, carried

After the Naming, the spoken name is on the banner, the save, the camp, and the loading of the second table. Before that, the company is unnamed and the game says so.

Done when a save file tells you whether the realm has spoken yet.

### 9. A codex

Marks, terrain, and a bestiary. An entry opens the first time you are cut by it, hidden by it, or hired beside it. The codex explains. It does not grant a stat.

Done when a new player can learn Ashbrand from the game.

### 10. Sound of the company

A cue for a step, a prime, a spend, a scar, a grave, and the camp at night. Distinct, short, and silent when the setting says so. No music that talks over a choice.

Done when you can tell a detonation from a miss without looking.

### 11. Two contracts before the Echo

The second table is three fights. Add two authored contracts between Hex Market and the Echo. Same grid, same three marks, pointed back at the company. Not a new march. Not a random map.

Done when the road from the Naming to the Echo takes more than an afternoon.

### 12. Foes that use the ground

Flyers ignore mud. A shot into forest can miss what it cannot see. Rubble is a gift to anything that shoots. A leader is the contract: the fight can end when the leader falls, if the contract says so.

Done when walking the open ground is a way to lose.

### 13. Captains with a tell

The Mouth, the Unnamed, the Echo, the Saint, the Wyrm. Each has one thing they will do that the grid shows before they do it. Not more health. A habit.

Done when you can bait a captain because you have seen the habit.

### 14. The radiant board

The five radiant contracts stay a fixed pool, not a generator. Camp offers a few. Ignoring one long enough lets it worsen: another body, a worse ground, the same foe. Taking it pays coin and a line in the chronicle. It never pays a new item.

Done when a radiant is a choice with a consequence, not a button that refills.

### 15. A night that costs something

At camp, one choice before the march. Rest the most hurt blade, and the next foe has had time to dig in. March now, and that blade goes out short of blood. Bury the dead, and the living walk lighter. You do not get all three.

Done when the morning is different because of the night.

### 16. The march map

The Pale Marches as a map, not a menu. The first table, the second table, and the radiant sites are places you can see. You still go only where a contract allows. The map shows where you have bled.

Done when the company has a geography.

### 17. Weather and the hour

A small set of states on maps that already exist: dusk, rain, ashfall. Each changes one rule you already know. Rain deepens mire. Ashfall shortens what forest hides. Dusk makes the first strike of a round harder to read. No new damage type.

Done when the same contract fought in the rain is a different fight.

### 18. The realm answers

After the Echo, if the company is still alive, four authored contracts. The realm has heard the name. Some foes spend your marks first. Some ground is already branded. Same rules, aimed at who you became. This is not a third system. It is the world talking back.

Done when surviving the Echo opens a road instead of a credits line.

### 19. Endings

Three ways the book closes, and the chronicle knows which. The name kept: the realm answers and the company walks out. The name buried: the Echo falls and no one is left to be answered. The grave: the company dies earlier, and the marches keep the blades. No secret rank. No new game plus that erases the scars.

Done when two finished saves can be different stories.

### 20. The browser, sealed

One path from the title to an ending, on a phone, installed, in landscape. Saves survive an update. Sprites are clean. The field fits. The codex, the camp, the map, and the chronicle agree with each other. A stranger can finish the Toll Road without being told the rules out loud.

Done when you would hand the address to someone who has not seen it built.

## After the seal

Unity may copy the browser, starting with a strike on the Toll Road. Not before phase 20. The browser does not wait on Unity, and Unity does not invent rules the browser does not already play.

## Not this

- Multiplayer. The company is one player's.
- A loot ladder, a shop of weapons, or a fourth mark.
- Rewriting the trees. The forks are exclusive on purpose.
- Procedural maps. New contracts are written, not rolled.
- A third march with new rules. Phase 18 is the realm answering, under the laws above.
- Unity, before the browser is sealed.

## How to use this

The next phase is 3. The fight you can read. The field fits, and the faces are theirs. A spectator should be able to name the acting blade, the mark, and the strike without the log.
